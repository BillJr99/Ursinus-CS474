## Try It: Learning Curves and the Power Law of Practice

Interfaces feel "intuitive" when practiced actions become automatic — and that speedup follows a predictable pattern, the power law of practice.  This notebook simulates a user practicing a UI task, fits the law to recover the learning rate, and models what happens when a redesign breaks a learned habit (Gourville's "curse of innovation" in miniature).  It pairs naturally with the World 1-1 case study above.

## Explore Further

- [Laws of UX - Jakob's Law](https://lawsofux.com/jakobs-law/) — "users spend most of their time on other sites": a one-page, beautifully illustrated statement of why matching existing habits makes designs feel intuitive.
- [Game Maker's Toolkit - Super Mario 3D World's 4 Step Level Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dBmIkEvEBtA) (video, ~12 min) — how Nintendo introduces a mechanic safely, develops it, twists it, and lets players demonstrate mastery.  Written summary: each level teaches by structured, low-stakes repetition — exactly the habit-building scaffold this activity asks you to design.
